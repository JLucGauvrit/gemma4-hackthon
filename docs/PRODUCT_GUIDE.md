# Product guide — Nutrition Disagreement Brief

Gemma 4 Hackathon (Alien open-science infra) · Working doc, built step by step toward the submission write-up.

## 1. The idea

Give a nutrition researcher, in 60 seconds, the map of disagreement on a claim: who says what, on what evidence, and why studies diverge.

Not a yes/no answer engine — a tool that surfaces why the science looks unsettled, sourced to real papers and preprints.

## 2. Scope

Five contested, well-documented claims only:

1. Saturated fat and cardiovascular risk
2. Eggs and cholesterol
3. Red meat
4. Sweeteners
5. Salt intake

Staying at five keeps the build tractable for the hackathon window and lets the team hand-tune quality instead of spreading thin.

## 4. Infrastructure available (Alien + Gemma 4)

- OpenAIRE MCP — research graph, 600M+ papers, citations, authors. No token needed.
- bioRxiv / medRxiv MCPs — full-text preprints, not just abstracts. No token needed.
- Alien backend — ingest custom sources, RAG engine, agentic workflows. Uses team access token.
- Metamorph — dynamic MCP, sources can be added/removed mid-conversation.
- workflow-engine plugin — compose agents/subagents into a full harness, exportable as an OpenAI-compatible API.

Judging criteria: grounded in real science, Gemma 4 does the reasoning, the system is agentic (acts, not just answers), every claim is traceable to a source.

Submission deadline: 20 Aug 2026. Deliverable: working artifact + short write-up.

## 5. The Brief — output specification

The core deliverable: a single structured answer, readable in under 60 seconds.

1. Claim restated — the question rephrased neutrally, to confirm the system understood it.
2. Disagreement verdict — not yes/no. A badge situating the claim on a spectrum: Established consensus / Contested / Emerging, limited evidence / Context-dependent.
3. Who says what — 2–3 camps found in the literature, each with: one-line position, dominant evidence type (RCT / observational / meta-analysis), relative strength of that evidence.
4. Why it diverges — the actual mechanics of disagreement: study design, population studied, what the variable is compared against (e.g. what saturated fat is replaced with), outcome definition (biomarker vs. hard clinical event), study duration, funding source.
5. Sources — every claim in the brief links to a real paper or preprint (OpenAIRE / bioRxiv / medRxiv), traceable and clickable.

Open question, still pending team decision: should the brief end with a practical takeaway for the researcher, or stay strictly neutral — map only, no conclusion?

## 6. Output format / support

Two different things, not to conflate:

- The product surface (what the researcher actually uses): a live web view, generated by the agent — matching how Alien's own demos work (demo.alien.club, openaire.demo.alien.club). This is what should run during the live judging, since agentic, does something scores higher than a static deck. A download as PDF option is worth adding so a researcher can save or share a brief.
- The design/prototyping tool (how Anaïs communicates the target UI to Adrian before it's built): a quick mockup is enough — no need for pixel-perfect Figma work under hackathon time pressure.
- The pitch deck (for judges, separate from the live demo): can be built later in Canva or Google Slides once the product story is locked — low priority until the artifact works.
